ElevenLabs Eleven v4: AI Voice That Takes Direction Mid-Sentence

ElevenLabs' most expressive voice model takes emotional direction mid-sentence — here's the full first look.

Updated: October 12, 2026

The 60-second verdict

ElevenLabs' Eleven v4 (launched September 28, 2026, alongside v4 Turbo) is the company's most expressive text-to-speech model yet: 90+ languages, voice cloning from as little as 10 seconds of audio, and — the headline feature — inline audio tags like `[laughs]`, `[whispers]`, and `[sighs]` that let you direct emotion inside a sentence, no separate editing pass. v4 Turbo trades a little quality for ~100ms latency aimed at voice agents. If your work is narration, ads, or character voice, v4 is the one to watch; if you build realtime voice agents, Turbo is the pick. Pricing is character-based, with a launch discount that ran through October 12 — check the current rates before budgeting.

What it is

ElevenLabs is the best-known AI voice company (reportedly valued around $22B in 2026 on ~$600M ARR pace). Eleven v4 is its flagship TTS model, successor to v3, built on a new architecture for context-aware emotional speech. It shipped with immediate availability across ElevenLabs' own surfaces — the ElevenCreative studio, ElevenAgents platform, and the ElevenAPI (model IDs `eleven_v4` and `eleven_v4_turbo`).

The big idea: previous TTS gave you one emotional setting per generation. v4 lets you direct delivery inline — emotion, pacing, even sound effects — written directly in the script, with the model also using surrounding context to adjust expression on its own.

How it works: the audio tags (first-look notes)

This is the feature everyone is talking about, so here's how it works per ElevenLabs' launch materials and early technical write-ups:

  • Inline direction tags. Write `[laughs]`, `[whispers]`, `[sighs]` — or free-form directions like `[strong French accent]` — directly in your text, placed right before the words they affect. v4 performs them instead of reading them aloud. (Older models like Flash v2.5 would literally say the word "laughs" — v4 acts it out.)
  • Stackable tags. v3 introduced single tags; v4 lets you stack multiple tags in sequence and follows their order, while using surrounding context to smooth the transitions — less manual annotation needed.
  • Sound effects. Beyond voice direction, v4 handles inline SFX cues, which matters for ads and character work.
  • 90+ languages, up from ~70 in v3. ElevenLabs reports the biggest quality jumps in Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese, Mandarin, and Cantonese.
  • Voice cloning from 10 seconds. Instant voice cloning (IVC) needs only ~10s of reference audio, and v4 holds the cloned identity more reliably across long texts than v3. Professional voice cloning (PVC) consistency is also improved.
  • v4 Turbo for realtime. Same expressive range, tuned for stream-in/stream-out: ~100ms median inference, ~150ms time-to-first-speech per ElevenLabs' figures — built for voice agents, support bots, and realtime calls.

One integration note for developers: v4 is served on ElevenLabs' text-to-dialogue streaming endpoint (not the older TTS websocket), so existing code needs small changes to adopt it.

ElevenLabs also rolled v4 into ElevenReader, its reading app — which now narrates PDFs, ebooks, and articles in 90+ languages (up from 32), with 10 hours/month of TTS uploads on the Free plan.

Pros & cons

Pros

  • Most expressive ElevenLabs model to date; inline emotion direction is a genuine workflow change, not a gimmick.
  • 90+ languages with notably better Asian-language quality (Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese).
  • 10-second voice cloning with better long-form identity stability.
  • v4 Turbo gives voice-agent builders sub-150ms speech with the same tag system.
  • Free tier of ElevenReader lets anyone hear v4 quality without paying.

Cons

  • It's still prompt-and-listen: directing well takes experimentation; tags are powerful but not magic.
  • API migration required (new endpoint, new model IDs) — not a drop-in upgrade for existing integrations.
  • Character-based pricing punishes long-form experimentation; budget before you binge.
  • Launch promo pricing has expired — the standard rates are 3–4× higher than the promo window.

Who it's for

  • Video creators & podcasters who need narration with actual acting in it — ads, explainers, character voices. This is v4's home turf, and the reason it leads our creator-audio picks.
  • Authors & publishers exploring audiobook narration: long-form identity stability is the specific v4 improvement to watch.
  • Voice-agent builders: skip straight to v4 Turbo and benchmark time-to-first-speech on your own stack — vendor latency slides are directional.
  • Multilingual teams: if your content ships in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, or Brazilian Portuguese, v4's reported quality jumps are worth an A/B test against v3.
  • Hold off if you need deterministic, broadcast-identical reads of the same script every time — expressive models trade some consistency for performance.

FAQ

When did Eleven v4 launch? September 28, 2026, announced on X with 2.2M+ views, alongside v4 Turbo. ElevenLabs cited a #1 rank from Artificial Analysis on launch day.

What's the difference between v4 and v4 Turbo? v4 is quality-first (audiobooks, ads, character work, long-form). v4 Turbo keeps the expressive range and tag system but is optimized for realtime streaming (~100ms inference) for voice agents.

How do the audio tags work? Place a tag like `[whispers]` immediately before the words it should affect. v4 performs the direction; you can stack tags and add free-form directions like `[strong French accent]`.

Can it clone my voice? Yes — instant cloning from ~10 seconds of reference audio, with better identity preservation across long texts than v3. Professional voice cloning is also improved.
